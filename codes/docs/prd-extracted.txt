CHAPTER 1 INTRODUCTION
1.1 Product Overview
The system supports patients, particularly international patients, through the administrative and operational stages of an outpatient hospital visit. It provides two patient channels: a React Native mobile application and a React.js web interface opened from hospital QR links. Both channels use the same patient records, reception decisions, Visits and Patient Journeys.
Patients prepare an appointment reference or walk-in information before approaching reception. Reception staff verifies the attendee, confirms registration and issues an examination ticket. That decision creates the Visit and prepares its Journey. The patient scans the ticket QR to activate or resume guidance. Doctors and authorized staff supply operational events; the system translates those events into understandable next actions.
Visit closure and guidance completion are separate. The Visit closes after required clinical work and final settlement are confirmed. If pharmacy guidance remains, the Journey continues until the patient confirms completion of the final guidance step. Published doctor summaries remain available according to account ownership or valid guest access, independently of either lifecycle.
1.2 Problem Statement
A patient may have an appointment but still be unsure how to obtain an examination ticket, find the consultation room, wait for a turn, complete additional services, return to the doctor, pay or collect medication. A patient without the application also needs immediate access to understandable reception instructions. After consultation, remembering the doctor explanation and care instructions is a separate need from knowing where to go next.
The product must maintain continuity from preparation to reception, guidance and later review. It must prevent repeated QR scans, account registration or changes between app and web from creating duplicate attendances or losing the current context.

1.4 Target Users
User

Reception and hospital staff

1.5 Stakeholders
Patients need understandable guidance and continuity of access. Hospital reception, service departments and finance personnel need their existing operational decisions to be reflected accurately. Doctors retain responsibility for clinical statements. Hospital administration governs content, access and integration arrangements. External service owners provide verified identity, appointment, queue, settlement or authorized content interfaces where those integrations are available.
1.6 Assumptions and Operational Dependencies
Hospital locations, supported services, workflow mappings and approved guidance resources are available for configuration.
Reception personnel can verify the attendee and confirm the administrative service/department before issuing an examination ticket.
Clinical/service progress and settlement outcomes come from authorized staff actions or supported integration events. A documented simulation may supply these events when a production interface is unavailable.
Doctor summaries originate from the responsible doctor or an authorized source reviewed before publication. Full clinical decision-making remains in the hospital process.
Session expiry, QR validity and data retention are governed by explicit deployed policies. Expiry affects access, not whether a stored Visit existed.
Patient web remains usable when an app link cannot be opened. Detailed indoor positioning and external voice availability are not prerequisites for readable guidance.
1.7 Product Scope
1.7.1 Included Capabilities
Patient app and patient web entry, language selection, local/external account access and an explicit guest option.
Appointment preparation, walk-in requests, multilingual reception communication, staff scanning and examination-ticket issuance.
One Visit/Journey pair per confirmed attendance, separate state transitions, configured Doctor Orders, queue/status guidance and basic navigation.
Authorized consultation/service and final-settlement events, followed by pharmacy guidance when required.
Doctor drafts and published diagnosis explanations/care instructions, approved translation handling, replayable narration and independent follow-up recommendations.
Assistance requests, account-linked history, valid guest-context review and optional verified guest-to-account conversion.
Administration of locations, services, mappings, approved operational templates, permissions and a reproducible delivery workflow.
1.7.2 Excluded Capabilities
Autonomous diagnosis, symptom-based clinical triage, prescribing or independent interpretation of results.
A replacement for the legal medical record, full hospital information system, laboratory/PACS system or prescribing/dispensing platform.
A pharmacy confirmation task added solely to close this guidance Journey, or a claim that patient guidance completion proves medication dispensing.
Production invoice calculation, payment collection, insurance adjudication or a native appointment-booking platform.
Unrestricted live medical conversation interpretation or recording every doctor consultation.
Precision indoor-positioning infrastructure and emergency-department clinical workflows.
CHAPTER 2 PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS
The functional and non-functional requirements define user-visible outcomes, responsibilities and verification conditions. Chapters 3 to 5 specify the corresponding architecture, state rules, workflows and use cases.

Requirement

FR-JRN-09

FR-JRN-10

2.3 Non Functional Requirements
2.3.1 Usability
ID
Requirement and verification condition
NFR-USAB-01
The active Journey screen shall show current status, next action, destination and wait/proceed guidance together. Acceptance: these four items are identifiable in every primary workflow scenario.
NFR-USAB-02
Listen, Navigate and Request Assistance shall be reachable within two interactions from the Journey screen. Guest entry shall be visible beside Sign in and Register; creating an account shall be optional.
2.3.2 Performance
ID
Requirement and verification condition
NFR-PERF-01
For a documented acceptance workload of 20 concurrent users and 10,000 stored Journey steps, the 95th-percentile response time for normal application-owned requests shall be at most two seconds. Record the environment and dataset used; external provider latency is measured separately.
NFR-PERF-02
External calls shall have a finite configured timeout and a visible loading, failure and retry state. A provider timeout shall preserve the last confirmed local workflow state.
2.3.3 Security and privacy
ID
Requirement and verification condition
NFR-SEC-01
The server shall check role, patient ownership or guest grant, and staff scope for every protected read and mutation. Acceptance includes denial of another patient record even when the requester has the same role.
NFR-SEC-02
Use HTTPS, protected session handling and restricted audit logging. QR payloads and URLs shall exclude personal and clinical text; redeemable opaque references require validation and shall not be logged as credentials.
NFR-PRIV-01
Collect only profile, reception, guidance and patient-facing clinical content required by this scope. Make guest access limits and the account-linking purpose visible to the patient.
NFR-PRIV-02
Keep legal medical records and unnecessary raw clinical results in hospital systems. Store only authorized summaries, instructions and references needed for this product, under a documented retention policy.
NFR-SEC-03
Account conversion shall verify both the guest context and target account, resist replay, renew the authenticated session and preserve resource links. A bare Visit identifier shall never authorize a claim.
2.3.4 Reliability and limited connectivity
ID
Requirement and verification condition
NFR-REL-01
Persist reception, Visit, Journey and publication state on the server. Reloading the page or restarting the app shall reconstruct the last confirmed progress.
NFR-REL-02
Process reception confirmation, settlement and workflow events idempotently and transactionally. A retry or concurrent submission shall not duplicate a Visit, ticket or order-generated step.
NFR-REL-03
During connectivity loss, retain previously loaded critical instructions when available and mark them as potentially stale. Do not confirm server-side clinical or settlement transitions from cached client data.
NFR-REL-04
A failed or cancelled account-conversion attempt shall preserve stored guest data and valid access. If the account exists but linking fails, support a safe retry rather than creating another account or Visit.
2.3.5 Accessibility
ID
Requirement and verification condition
NFR-ACC-01
Support text scaling, meaningful control labels and keyboard-accessible patient web actions. Guidance and doctor-content screens shall provide clear playback and stop controls.
NFR-ACC-02
Every critical spoken instruction shall have equivalent readable text. Audio failure shall not block reception, navigation, guidance or access to published doctor content.
2.3.6 Localization
ID
Requirement and verification condition
NFR-LOC-01
Support Unicode and separate externalized interface text from approved guidance/doctor-content translations. Vietnamese and English shall cover the baseline end-to-end acceptance scenarios; additional languages require published content resources.
NFR-LOC-02
Allow preferred-language changes during guest or account use without creating a new patient identity, Visit or Journey.
NFR-LOC-03
Resolve medical translations only against their approved source version. Templates and custom doctor content shall retain separate authorship and publication controls.
NFR-LOC-04
Show the actual content language and fallback state whenever the selected language is unavailable; retain access to the authorized original source.
2.3.7 Maintainability and compatibility
ID
Requirement and verification condition
NFR-MNT-01
Keep supported service mappings, hospital destinations, content templates and role permissions configurable and validated before publication.
NFR-MNT-02
Separate business responsibilities into backend modules while keeping one deployable application and centralized transition rules.
NFR-COMP-01
Provide the patient app with React Native, and patient web plus staff/doctor/cashier/admin web interfaces with React.js. Verify the primary flows on the supported mobile and desktop browser matrix recorded for each release.
NFR-DEL-01
Maintain a reproducible repository build and an automated integration/delivery pipeline that runs applicable checks and produces deployable web/backend artifacts and the supported mobile build. Keep deployment secrets outside source code.

CHAPTER 3 SYSTEM DESIGN
3.1 System Architecture Overview
The system uses a modular monolith: one deployable backend application with separated identity, reception, Visit, Journey, clinical-content and support responsibilities. Patient app and patient web are two clients of the same application data and authorization model. Staff, doctor, cashier and administrator views access permitted operations through the backend API.
The workflow module is the authority for transition rules. The Visit module records the attendance and closure outcome; the Journey module records guidance activation and completion. Closing a Visit does not make its unfinished pharmacy Journey inaccessible. Clinical content is linked to the Visit and can be reviewed independently of Journey status.

3.4.2 Relationships and Integrity
A Patient may have zero or one local PatientAccount, multiple verified external links and multiple guest sessions. A verified account conversion attaches access to the existing patient record; identity conflicts require staff resolution rather than an unverified merge.
A confirmed ReceptionRecord refers to exactly one reception source: an Appointment or a RegistrationRequest. One reception record creates one Visit. Each Visit has one ExaminationTicket and one PatientJourney.
A Visit belongs to one Patient and may reference one Appointment. Enforce patient consistency across the reception source, Visit, ticket and access grants.
A Journey contains multiple steps. A DoctorOrder can generate multiple steps; order_id is nullable for steps such as initial consultation or final pharmacy guidance that were not created by a service order.
Step prerequisites, services and locations are validated. An order update addresses its linked steps explicitly rather than matching only by service name.
DoctorSummary versions and approved translations belong to the Visit through a stable summary reference. Follow-up recommendations are separate records; they are not required for diagnosis/instruction visibility.
Access grants, QR redemption and account-conversion claims are checked server-side. Code reissue, retry and account linking preserve Visit and Journey keys.
VisitEvent uniqueness by source and source_event_id prevents replay effects. Reception confirmation, order/step changes and guarded closure use transactions or equivalent consistency controls.

3.6 Localization and Narration Architecture
Interface text, operational guidance and doctor-published clinical content are separate resources. Interface labels can be localized independently. Operational guidance uses approved event templates. Clinical translations must be approved against the exact published source version; no unreviewed explanation is substituted for what the doctor confirmed.
Resolution uses approved selected-language content first, approved English second and the Vietnamese source when available. If the authorized original uses another language and no approved fallback exists, show that original with its language label and assistance options. The screen always identifies a fallback. Vietnamese and English are the baseline acceptance languages; enabling an additional language requires the relevant published resources.
TTS consumes the resolved permitted text. Cache keys include content identity, source version and language. Publishing a correction invalidates obsolete current-audio references. A TTS failure leaves readable text available. Doctor-content replay remains possible after Visit closure and Journey completion as long as the user remains authorized.
3.7 Navigation Architecture
Navigation receives a destination from the Journey service and resolves a configured route, textual direction or known department/floor/room. A known starting point may be selected when required. The product does not depend on precise indoor GPS. Pharmacy guidance follows the same mechanism even when Visit.status is Closed.
3.8 Authentication and Authorization
Patient identity, account authentication and Visit access are separate. A guest session can authorize a limited context without creating a local account. An account can retrieve only its linked records; staff must satisfy both role and assignment/department scope. Possession of a Visit identifier, ticket photograph or public hospital QR alone does not authorize access to doctor content.
When an examination-ticket link is opened on another device or by an unmatched account, the system preserves the intended target but requires matching account authentication, the original valid guest context or staff-assisted verification. Reception can reissue access after checking the attendee. An expired access code affects access, not the existence of the Visit.
Guest conversion stores a protected continuation context before navigation to registration. The server verifies the guest claim and target account, links existing records, renews authenticated access and consumes the successful claim. Failed registration or linking preserves data and valid guest access. If an account was created before linking failed, a retry authenticates that account instead of creating another one.

Published-only content, author/time, original and approved fallback language, exact source version, audio identity/status and non-blocking provider errors.
Updates
Document polling, SSE or WebSocket choice, refresh interval/reconnection and ordering/version rules. Transport choice remains unresolved until backend agreement.
Simulation
Deterministic fixtures/adapters for appointment, queue, service and settlement events; same IDs, states and error contracts as the integration represented. Label simulated sources in demo documentation.
Organize frontend code by feature with reusable presentation components, an API client, session/context handling, navigation and localization resources. Share types, validation rules and domain helpers between channels where compatible; Web DOM and Native UI controls remain platform-specific. Backend responses are authoritative for actionable steps and lifecycle transitions. Refresh after mutations and reconnect; discard outdated responses when Visit, locale or content version changes.
Define request timeouts and cancellation behavior. Authentication expiry preserves the intended target and offers verified recovery without deleting records. A conflict reloads the latest confirmed record and explains the rejected action. Missing mapping, unverified access or provider failure must not be converted into a fabricated successful workflow result.
3.15 Connectivity Localization and Narration States
Every primary screen shall specify loading, empty, ready, failed and retry states. Protected screens shall also define denied and expired access. During connectivity loss, previously loaded critical instructions remain available only where permitted by the access policy, with their update time and potentially stale status displayed. Unconfirmed clinical or settlement mutations shall not be reported as complete. Sensitive cache persistence, clearing on sign-out or context change, and expiry behavior require an explicit security agreement.
Externalize interface strings independently from approved operational and doctor-content resources. Vietnamese and English cover the baseline acceptance flow. Show actual language and fallback explicitly and retain the authorized original. Language changes preserve identity and Journey progress. Playback is patient-initiated with Play, Stop and Replay; diagnosis/explanation and care instructions have separate controls. On source-version or language change, stop obsolete playback and resolve matching text/audio. Audio unavailability leaves readable text and a retry option.
3.16 Frontend Build Delivery and Verification
Record supported Android/iOS targets, OS versions, browsers, devices/viewports, Web hosting, mobile distribution and environment names before release acceptance. These deployment selections remain open until the team records them; producing a supported mobile artifact does not imply publication to a public application store.
For pull requests and integration commits, the automated pipeline shall install locked dependencies, run applicable lint/type checks and meaningful component/integration tests, and build the Web client. The supported mobile build runs on the configured compatible runner. Failed required checks block release. A release pipeline deploys the approved Web artifact to its configured environment, generates/distributes the supported mobile artifact and runs a smoke check. Record the trigger, approval/environment rules, artifact retention and rollback/redeployment procedure. Backend jobs follow NFR-DEL-01 and are coordinated with the assigned backend owner.
Keep provider credentials and signing/deployment secrets in protected pipeline/environment storage, outside source code and client bundles. Only non-secret public configuration such as the API base URL may be shipped to clients. Document setup, build/test commands, environment variables, simulation use, demo access and release evidence in the repository README.

CHAPTER 4 BUSINESS WORKFLOW
4.1 Overall Patient Journey
Both patient channels converge at the same reception decision. Preparation produces a reception QR, not a Visit. Reception confirmation produces the Visit and examination ticket. Ticket scanning starts or resumes guidance. Authorized clinical/service events advance the operational path, final settlement closes the Visit, and pharmacy guidance may continue independently. Published doctor information remains accessible under the patient access rules.

4.2 Hospital Arrival and Entry
A patient with the app signs in or registers, reusing a valid existing session. Linking an external appointment account is optional. A patient arriving without the app scans the general hospital QR and enters patient web. The unauthenticated entry screen presents language selection and Sign in, Register and Continue as guest. Supported associated links may open an installed app; otherwise the web remains usable.
Guest entry follows the standard walk-in preparation path. A guest who has an appointment from another source may present its reference to reception for staff verification. A ticket QR preserves its ticket-access purpose and is not handled as a new walk-in registration.

4.3 Reception Confirmation and Ticket Issuance
Booked reception. The app retrieves verified appointments; the patient chooses the intended booking and receives a reception QR. Staff scans it, validates the booking and confirms the attendee. Valid booked reception proceeds to ticket issuance without repeating the whole walk-in form.
Walk-in reception. The patient enters the required personal/contact information, condition description and requests on app or web. The system saves the request and displays a reception QR. Staff reviews it, asks for missing information through approved communication, and confirms the administrative service/department. Supplements update the same request.
Common confirmation. Staff confirms reception. The system atomically records the reception outcome, creates one Active Visit, prepares one Pending Activation Journey and issues the examination ticket with its QR. Department, room and queue details are associated when available. Repeated confirmation and printing retries reuse those records.
4.4 Journey Activation and Queue Guidance
Patient scans the examination-ticket QR. The app or web verifies access and opens the existing Journey. A late scan loads the latest progress already recorded by the hospital workflow. The active screen shows current status, action, destination and wait/proceed guidance, with queue information and its update time where available. Repeated scans never restart the attendance.
If the ticket is opened by an unmatched account or a new browser without the original guest context, the system preserves the target and requests verified sign-in or staff-assisted recovery. Scanning a ticket photograph alone does not grant access to another patient clinical content.
4.5 Examination and Doctor Orders
The patient follows consultation-room and queue instructions. The responsible doctor opens the assigned Visit. Clinical consultation occurs under the hospital professional process; the guidance system records the operational completion and supported orders needed to determine what the patient should do next.
An accepted Doctor Order creates linked steps for supported services. Order changes update eligible remaining steps; completed valid facts are retained. Missing mappings reject the new executable order and require configuration/staff resolution. Consultation completion alone does not close the Visit when required services or a return consultation remain.
4.6 Laboratory Imaging and Return Consultation
For each supported order, the patient sees the service destination and approved operational instruction. Service staff or a trusted source records progress/completion. If a return consultation is required, it becomes actionable after all required preceding services are resolved. The responsible doctor then completes that consultation and determines whether more services, a published summary or follow-up are required.
Patients cannot mark examinations or clinical services complete themselves. Multiple ordered services follow validated prerequisites and the configured operational sequence. The system shows an unresolved configuration state instead of inventing a route or treating missing data as completion.
4.7 Doctor Diagnosis Summary and Care Instructions
The doctor opens Results and Care Instructions for the Visit and prepares three separate patient-facing sections: diagnosis and optional plain-language explanation; care instructions; and an optional follow-up recommendation. An existing authorized prescription/instruction document can be referenced, without adding a prescribing function.
Incomplete content remains Draft. After review, the doctor publishes it, optionally through a combined Complete Consultation and Publish Summary action. Published content is available immediately in the current Visit; payment and Journey completion are not prerequisites. No follow-up recommendation is needed for the diagnosis/instructions to remain visible.
The patient can read the current approved text and listen to diagnosis/explanation and instructions separately. Narration reads what the doctor confirmed or its approved translation; if no explanatory text was provided, the system does not invent one. An authorized correction produces a new version, updates the patient display and invalidates obsolete narration. Access continues through account history or a valid guest grant.
4.8 Payment and Visit Closure
When required consultations and services have been resolved, the Journey guides the patient to final settlement. The authorized cashier/source confirms final payment or a no-payment-required outcome. The system records the outcome and closes the Visit automatically once all closure conditions hold. Invoice calculation and payment collection remain in the hospital system.
An intermediate service payment does not close the Visit. If final-settlement confirmation arrives before all clinical conditions are resolved, the Visit remains Active until those conditions and current settlement eligibility are verified. Closed means that the tracked clinical and settlement work is finished; it is not a record that medication has been dispensed.
4.9 Pharmacy Guidance and Journey Completion
If the confirmed workflow requires medication collection, the patient is guided to the pharmacy after final settlement. This Journey remains usable even though the Visit is Closed. The pharmacy employee has no additional action in the guidance system solely to confirm Journey completion.
At the eligible final pharmacy guidance step, the patient chooses Complete Journey. The system records completion of guidance by the patient, not an official dispensing event. If no pharmacy guidance remains when the Visit closes, the system completes the Journey automatically. Closing the app/browser leaves an unfinished Journey resumable.
4.10 Guest Account Conversion and History
After Journey completion, a guest sees an optional invitation to create an account to review visit history, with a Skip option. The Visit and its content were already stored at reception and publication time. Registration adds verified account access to those existing records rather than saving a new copy of the Visit.
Before opening registration, the server retains a protected continuation context. Successful registration/sign-in verifies the guest claim, links the existing patient and records, renews authenticated access and returns to the same Visit/history. Registration or linking failure preserves records and remaining valid guest access. A patient who registers earlier resumes the current step. If access expires, verified recovery is required; expiry alone does not delete the stored Visit.
4.11 Follow-up Attendance
The patient can review a doctor recommendation separately from the diagnosis and care instructions. Where supported, an external booking handoff can be opened. A recommendation is not displayed as a confirmed appointment until the appointment source confirms it. On the future attendance, reception creates a new Visit and Journey; the prior records remain historical.

CHAPTER 5 USE CASE ANALYSIS AND SPECIFICATION

Patient covers authenticated account use and restricted guest use. The system consumes authorized outcomes from those processes rather than claiming to replace them.

Brief Description
This use case begins when an unauthenticated patient opens the app/web entry or chooses account access. The system establishes account or guest access while retaining the selected language and intended entry destination.

A valid account session or guest session is established. No Visit is created by this use case.

Brief Description
This use case begins when an administrator needs to publish a configuration change. It maintains validated configuration used by reception, Journey, navigation, localization, and staff authorization.
Precondition
The administrator is authenticated with configuration permissions. Medical source content remains doctor-owned.
Main flow
1. Administrator opens the relevant configuration area.
2. Administrator updates departments, locations, services, or supported workflow mappings.
3. Administrator maintains approved operational templates, reception messages, and language resources.
4. Administrator updates staff roles and departmental scope when authorized.
5. System validates dependencies, destinations, completion authority, content publication, and active references.
6. Administrator publishes the valid configuration version and the system records an audit event.
Alternative flow
A1 - Service or location disabled: New selection is prevented while active and historical references are preserved.
A2 - Translation not approved: It remains unpublished and the existing approved fallback is used.
A3 - Missing mapping, broken prerequisite, or unsafe active-record reference: Publication is rejected.
A4 - Administrator attempts to change a doctor diagnosis as administrator: The clinical publication action is denied.
Post condition
A valid configuration version is published, or an invalid change is rejected without breaking active or historical references.
UC-13 Enter through Hospital QR
Use-case
Enter through Hospital QR
Actors
Patient; Device Operating System and Link Handler
Brief Description
This use case begins when the patient scans the public hospital guidance QR. It opens the correct patient entry interface and preserves the QR-purpose and language context.
Precondition
The QR resolves to the configured hospital entry URL.
Main flow
1. Device opens the hospital link in the installed associated app when supported; otherwise it opens patient web.
2. System identifies the public-entry purpose and presents language selection.
3. System reuses a valid signed-in session or presents Sign in, Register, and Continue as guest.
4. Patient establishes access through UC-01.
5. System continues to booked preparation available to a linked account or the standard walk-in path.
Alternative flow
A1 - Browser retains the link despite an installed app: Web remains usable and an Open in app option may be offered.
A2 - Existing unfinished Journey: An authorized signed-in patient or valid guest context is given access without creating another attendance.
A3 - Malformed or unsupported hospital link: A clear error and staff-contact option are shown.
A4 - Authentication failure: Entry context is retained and retry or guest access is offered.
Post condition
The installed app or patient web displays entry in the selected language. No Visit is created.
UC-14 Confirm Reception and Issue Examination Ticket
Use-case
Confirm Reception and Issue Examination Ticket
Actors
Receptionist; Patient; External Appointment System (when a booking is checked)
Brief Description
This use case begins when reception staff scans a patient reception QR or checks a supplied booking reference. The receptionist confirms the actual attendance and issues the one examination ticket that identifies the Visit and Journey.
Precondition
Reception staff is authorized. A valid booking/request context and the attendee can be verified.
Main flow
1. Receptionist scans the reception QR and the system validates its purpose, validity, and associated patient context.
2. System retrieves the booked appointment or persisted walk-in Registration Request.
3. For booked reception, staff verifies the booking and attendee. For walk-in reception, staff reviews information and confirms the administrative routing/service.
4. Receptionist confirms reception and ticket issuance.
5. System atomically records the reception outcome, creates the Active Visit, and prepares its Pending Activation Journey.
6. System associates known department, room, and queue details and issues the examination ticket with the Journey QR.
7. Receptionist gives the ticket to the patient and explains that scanning its QR activates guidance.
Alternative flow
A1 - Incomplete walk-in information: UC-08 updates the same Registration Request and returns it for review before any Visit is created.
A2 - Guest presents an external booking reference: Staff verifies it and uses the booked branch when valid.
A3 - Reception source already confirmed: Existing Visit and ticket are returned instead of creating duplicates.
A4 - Ticket/access code needs reissue: Staff verifies identity and reissues access against the same Visit and Journey.
A5 - Wrong-purpose, expired, or invalid code: It is rejected and a supported recovery route is offered.
A6 - Booking identity/status cannot be confirmed: Reception remains unresolved and staff resolution is required.
A7 - Persistence failure: Successful issuance is not reported and no partial active Visit/Journey pair is left.
A8 - Printing failure after successful persistence: The same ticket is reprinted without another Visit.
Post condition
One Active Visit, one Pending Activation Journey, and one examination ticket exist. Repeated confirmation returns the same records.
UC-15 Activate or Resume Patient Journey
Use-case
Activate or Resume Patient Journey
Actors
Patient; Reception Staff for verified recovery
Brief Description
This use case begins when the patient scans the examination-ticket QR or opens its retained authorized link. It grants the correct patient context access to the existing Journey and shows current progress.
Precondition
The ticket references an existing Visit and Journey. The claimant has a matching authenticated identity, valid original guest context, or completed staff-assisted verification.
Main flow
1. System routes the ticket link to app or web and preserves its activation purpose.
2. System validates the ticket code, its status, and the claimant account/guest relationship.
3. System establishes or reuses the permitted Visit access grant.
4. System loads the existing Visit and Journey, including events recorded before the scan.
5. If Pending Activation with remaining guidance, the system activates the Journey; otherwise it retains the current state.
6. System opens current guidance through UC-04 or the completed record through UC-10.
Alternative flow
A1 - Sign-in or verified recovery needed: The target is preserved and reopened after successful verification.
A2 - Visit Closed with pharmacy guidance remaining: The remaining guidance is activated or resumed.
A3 - Code already redeemed by the same permitted context: Existing progress is resumed without reset.
A4 - Journey already Completed: Its record and permitted published content are shown.
A5 - Another account presents the code without ownership verification: Access is denied and staff resolution is offered.
A6 - Code expired or superseded: Reception may reissue it after verification without another Visit.
A7 - Network failure: Existing records are preserved and retry is offered.
Post condition
The same Journey is activated or resumed; a completed Journey is displayed as completed. No new Visit or Journey is created.
UC-16 Link Guest Records to an Account
Use-case
Link Guest Records to an Account
Actors
Guest Patient; External Identity Provider (when used); Reception Staff for identity conflicts
Brief Description
This use case begins when a guest accepts the optional account invitation after Journey completion or chooses registration/sign-in earlier. It links existing guest records to an account without losing the current context.
Precondition
The guest context can be verified and linked records already exist on the server. Registration/sign-in is available; the target account will be authenticated before any record is linked.
Main flow
1. System creates a protected continuation context for the current guest records and intended return destination.
2. Patient opens registration and completes it, or signs in to an existing account.
3. Server verifies both the guest claim and target account and checks identity-link conflicts.
4. System links the existing patient record and its request, Visit, Journey, and content access to the account in one consistent operation.
5. System renews authenticated access and consumes the successful claim; prior guest access is retired only after the link is safely established.
6. System returns the patient to the same current Journey or Visit/history page.
Alternative flow
A1 - Guest declines invitation: No account is created and valid current-session access continues.
A2 - Patient cancels or navigates back: The original valid guest context is restored.
A3 - Account already exists: Sign-in and claim verification are used instead of creating a duplicate account.
A4 - Registration before Journey completion: The current step resumes instead of being marked complete.
A5 - Registration/linking fails: Existing records are preserved and a safe retry is provided; an already-created account is reused after authentication.
A6 - Guest access expired or ownership conflict: Verified recovery is required rather than trusting a Visit identifier.
A7 - Claim replay: The already established authorized result is returned or unauthorized reuse is denied.
Post condition
Verified records are linked to the target account and the same Visit/Journey is reopened; otherwise records and remaining valid guest access are preserved.
UC-17 Record Consultation and Service Progress
Use-case
Record Consultation and Service Progress
Actors
Authorized Doctor or Service Staff; Supported Queue/Service Source
Brief Description
This use case begins when an assigned consultation, service, or queue event occurs. It records operational facts that determine the next valid patient step and clinical readiness for final settlement.
Precondition
The Visit is Active. The actor/source is authorized for the event and its relevant Journey step or service.
Main flow
1. Authorized actor opens the assigned Visit/service, or the trusted adapter supplies an event.
2. System validates the source, allowed transition, current state, and event identity.
3. Actor/source reports the permitted progress or completion outcome.
4. System updates the operational record and corresponding Journey steps, retaining audit details.
5. Workflow service resolves prerequisites, including completion of all required services before a required return consultation.
6. When all required clinical work is resolved, the system marks final settlement actionable and evaluates any verified current final-settlement outcome.
7. System refreshes patient guidance and available queue information.
Alternative flow
A1 - Doctor completes consultation and publishes patient content: UC-20 may be performed in the same user action.
A2 - Authorized cancellation/skip: A service is resolved under its allowed rule and the reason/actor is retained.
A3 - Valid final-settlement outcome already exists: UC-18 closure guards are evaluated.
A4 - Patient attempts clinical/service confirmation: The action is denied.
A5 - Invalid transition or duplicate event: The invalid change is rejected or the prior confirmed result is returned.
A6 - Required work remains: The Visit stays open and final closure is not unlocked.
Post condition
A valid event and resulting transitions are persisted, or the event is rejected without claiming completion.
UC-18 Record Final Settlement and Close Visit
Use-case
Record Final Settlement and Close Visit
Actors
Authorized Cashier or Settlement Source; Patient receiving updated status
Brief Description
This use case begins when the final settlement outcome is confirmed, or clinical readiness changes after a verified current settlement event. It records final settlement and closes an eligible Visit without requiring pharmacy confirmation.
Precondition
The Visit exists; the source is authorized; settlement is identified as final for the current completed work or as an authorized no-payment-required outcome.
Main flow
1. Cashier/source submits the final outcome for the correct Visit.
2. System validates source authority, event identity, and the distinction between intermediate and final settlement.
3. System records the confirmed outcome without implementing invoice calculation or payment processing.
4. Workflow service checks that required consultations/services are resolved and the settlement applies to the current work.
5. If eligible, the system marks the Visit Closed and records the closure event and time.
6. System makes pharmacy guidance available when required and preserves the Journey activation state; otherwise it completes the Journey through UC-19.
7. Patient sees the updated status and next instruction.
Alternative flow
A1 - Intermediate payment: Only the relevant step is updated and the Visit remains Active.
A2 - Authorized no-payment-required outcome: The same clinical completion guard is applied.
A3 - Required work remains: The verified outcome is retained, the Visit remains Active, and closure is re-evaluated when remaining conditions are resolved.
A4 - Same final event already processed: Existing closure is returned without duplicate events.
A5 - Untrusted or mismatched event: The event is rejected.
A6 - New work invalidates earlier settlement eligibility: Current authorized revalidation is required before closure.
A7 - Persistence failure: Successful closure is not reported and an idempotent retry is offered.
Post condition
Settlement is recorded. The Visit becomes Closed only if all closure guards pass; pharmacy guidance remains accessible when required.
UC-19 Complete Patient Journey
Use-case
Complete Patient Journey
Actors
Patient (final pharmacy guidance branch); Authorized Final-Settlement Source (automatic no-pharmacy branch)
Brief Description
This use case begins when the patient chooses Complete Journey at the final pharmacy-guidance step, or when a Visit closes with no remaining pharmacy guidance. It completes the guidance record independently from Visit closure.
Precondition
The Visit is Closed. All required operational steps are resolved. Any remaining patient-confirmable step is the final pharmacy-guidance step.
Main flow
1. System checks the Visit closure and remaining guidance steps.
2. If pharmacy guidance remains, the system shows its destination and the Complete Journey action at the eligible final step.
3. Patient confirms completion of that guidance, or the system uses the no-pharmacy automatic branch.
4. System records Journey completion and its source; patient confirmation is not recorded as an official medication-dispensing event.
5. System offers published doctor information and any follow-up recommendation.
6. For a guest, the system offers optional account creation through UC-16 with a Skip option.
Alternative flow
A1 - No pharmacy guidance required: The Journey completes automatically after valid Visit closure.
A2 - Guest skips registration: Current authorized access remains available while valid.
A3 - Patient closes the client before completion: The unfinished Journey is preserved for resumption.
A4 - Visit still Active or required operational work unresolved: Premature completion is rejected.
A5 - Connection failure: The last confirmed state is retained and retry occurs without falsely reporting success.
A6 - Another patient context attempts completion: The mutation is denied.
Post condition
The Journey becomes Completed with a completion source and timestamp. The Visit stays Closed. Optional guest registration is offered.
UC-20 Publish Diagnosis Summary and Care Instructions
Use-case
Publish Diagnosis Summary and Care Instructions
Actors
Doctor responsible for the Visit; Approved Clinical Source (when integration is available)
Brief Description
This use case begins when the responsible doctor prepares the consultation conclusion or corrects previously published patient content. It publishes a reviewed patient-facing diagnosis summary, explanation, and care instructions that can be read and narrated.
Precondition
The doctor is authorized for the Visit. Content comes from the doctor, an approved template, or an authorized source requiring doctor review.
Main flow
1. Doctor opens Results and Care Instructions for the correct Visit.
2. Doctor enters the diagnosis summary, optional plain-language explanation, and care instructions, or adapts approved material.
3. Doctor saves a Draft while information is incomplete and reviews the content before release.
4. Doctor chooses Publish to Patient, optionally combined with completing consultation.
5. System validates authority and creates a Published version with its source, author, and timestamp.
6. System links approved translations to that exact source version and invalidates obsolete narration after a correction.
7. System notifies the patient that the content can be opened immediately through UC-21.
Alternative flow
A1 - Authorized hospital source supplies content: The doctor reviews it before publication rather than re-entering all medical records.
A2 - Results pending: Content remains Draft and is not exposed to the patient.
A3 - Correction of already published content, including after Visit closure: A new version is published without reopening the Visit.
A4 - No approved target-language version: The original and controlled fallback are preserved.
A5 - Existing authorized prescription/instruction document referenced: No prescribing function is invoked.
A6 - Unauthorized doctor or wrong patient context: Publication is prevented.
A7 - Invalid publication data or save failure: The previous published version remains authoritative and the new draft stays private.
A8 - Translation not approved against the source version: It is not published as an approved medical translation.
Post condition
A Draft remains private, or a new Published version becomes patient-accessible with authorship, time, and approved-language links.
UC-21 Review Diagnosis Summary and Care Instructions
Use-case
Review Diagnosis Summary and Care Instructions
Actors
Patient using an account or valid guest grant; Text-to-Speech Provider; External Booking Source (when follow-up handoff is chosen)
Brief Description
This use case begins when the patient opens Results and Care Instructions from the current Visit, a publication notification, or history. It allows review and replay of doctor-confirmed information independently of Visit/Journey completion and follow-up booking.
Precondition
The patient has access to the Visit. The selected doctor content is Published; absence of published content is represented explicitly.
Main flow
1. System authorizes the account or guest grant for the selected Visit.
2. System loads the current published diagnosis/explanation, care instructions, and any separate follow-up recommendation.
3. System displays author, publication/update time, the approved selected language or labelled fallback, and access to the original.
4. Patient reads the diagnosis and care instructions as separate sections.
5. Patient may invoke UC-05 to listen, stop, or replay either section.
6. Patient may return to an unfinished Journey or retain access through the permitted Visit/history context.
Alternative flow
A1 - No follow-up recommendation: Diagnosis and care instructions continue to be displayed.
A2 - No summary published yet: Not yet published is shown and no clinical content is synthesized.
A3 - Patient chooses Book Follow-up: The supported external booking handoff opens; a confirmed appointment is shown only after source confirmation.
A4 - Journey Completed and patient is a guest: UC-16 is offered while the grant remains valid.
A5 - Access expired or belongs to another patient: Disclosure is denied and verified recovery is offered.
A6 - TTS failure: Readable text remains available with a non-blocking retry.
A7 - Content correction supersedes a cached version: The latest approved content is loaded and obsolete audio is invalidated.
A8 - Booking integration unavailable: The recommendation is retained and available booking instructions are shown.
Post condition
Current authorized text and optional matching narration are presented. No new medical explanation, Visit, or booking is inferred.
